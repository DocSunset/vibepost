# Vibepost Competitive Analysis — Social Media Scheduling & Cross-Posting

**Prepared:** June 2026  
**Scope:** Social media scheduling and cross-posting tools, with emphasis on platforms relevant to vibepost (Facebook, Instagram, Threads, LinkedIn, Bluesky)  
**Pricing note:** All prices are sourced from live websites or well-dated third-party pricing trackers as of May–June 2026. Where only annual billing prices were available, the monthly equivalent is shown and noted. Where prices are in EUR, conversion is approximate and noted. A small number of entries rely on cached data from late 2025; these are flagged.

## 2. Value Offering Analysis

### Hootsuite
Hootsuite is the category incumbent, founded in 2008, and positions itself as a unified command centre for large marketing teams and enterprises. It covers publishing and scheduling, a social listening engine (via Talkwalker integration), unified inbox, analytics dashboards, bulk scheduling (up to 350 posts), competitor benchmarking, and extensive third-party integrations (100+). As of 2026, Hootsuite supports Facebook, Instagram, X/Twitter, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Threads, WhatsApp, Pinterest, and Bluesky (natively added in 2025). Its primary strength is breadth and enterprise credibility. Its primary weaknesses are its high price point ($99–$249+/user/month) and a UI that many users describe as complex and dated. It is not open source and offers no meaningful free tier (only a 30-day trial).

### Sprout Social
Sprout Social is the premium analytics play in the market, targeting mid-market to enterprise marketing teams who need deep reporting, CRM integration, and sophisticated approval workflows. Its 2026 pricing ranges from $79/seat/month (Essentials) to $399/seat/month (Advanced), with per-seat costs making it the most expensive mainstream option for teams. Key differentiators include the best-in-class reporting suite, sentiment analysis, CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot) integrations at higher tiers, and a well-regarded customer support experience. Platform coverage is strong: Facebook, Instagram, Threads, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, Pinterest, YouTube. Bluesky support was added in 2025. There is no free tier. Annual billing is required for the advertised rates.

### Agorapulse
Agorapulse occupies the mid-market sweet spot between the enterprise complexity of Hootsuite/Sprout and the simplicity of Buffer. It is particularly strong for agencies and teams that need a powerful unified inbox (for managing comments, DMs, and mentions), scheduling, and reporting—without enterprise pricing. Its 2026 plans start at $79/month (for one user, 10 profiles, annual) and include Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, X, YouTube, Threads, Pinterest, Bluesky, Google Business, and Reddit. Bluesky was added relatively early compared to peers. The free plan (3 profiles, 10 posts/month) is more functional than most competitors' free offerings. Per-user pricing makes team expansion costly.

### Buffer
Buffer has successfully repositioned itself as the approachable, affordable, developer-friendly scheduler for independent creators, small businesses, and technically-aware users. Its free tier (3 channels, 10 scheduled posts each) is the most generous free offering in the industry and supports Bluesky and Mastodon, reflecting Buffer's explicit philosophy of supporting open/decentralised social networks. Buffer was one of the first tools approved as a Bluesky publishing partner. Paid plans start at just $5/month per channel (Essentials) or $10/month per channel (Team, with unlimited team members). Supported platforms: Bluesky, Facebook, Google Business, Instagram, LinkedIn, Mastodon, Pinterest, Threads, TikTok, X, YouTube (11 total). The per-channel pricing model is unusual but can become expensive for users with many accounts. Analytics are solid but not enterprise-grade.

### Later
Later began as an Instagram-only visual planner and still carries that DNA strongly: its visual drag-and-drop calendar, Instagram grid preview, and link-in-bio tools remain its best-in-class features. It has expanded to Facebook, TikTok, Threads, YouTube Shorts, Pinterest, LinkedIn, and Snapchat, but has not added Bluesky support. Later targets lifestyle brands, influencers, and e-commerce businesses where visual presentation matters most. The 2026 lineup starts at $18.75/month (annual; Starter plan, 1 social set / 8 profiles). There is no free tier—only a 14-day trial. The platform is increasingly AI-forward with Smart Scheduling and Future Trends features. Bluesky's absence is a deliberate product choice that leaves a gap for creator-focused competitors.

### SocialPilot
SocialPilot is firmly positioned as the agency and freelancer value option: strong bulk scheduling (up to 500 posts at once, more than any competitor), white-label reports on higher tiers, and client approval workflows, all at prices that significantly undercut Hootsuite and Sprout. Its 2026 pricing starts at $17/month (annual; 5 accounts, 1 user) up to $170/month (40 accounts, unlimited users). All tiers support Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, Threads, YouTube, Pinterest, Google Business, and Bluesky. AI credits are capped by tier. SocialPilot's main limitation is that its product design shows its age—less polished than Buffer or Later—and analytics depth is behind Sprout and Agorapulse.

### Sendible
Sendible focuses squarely on agencies that need robust client-facing tools: white-label dashboards, client approval workflows, priority inbox, and custom reporting. Its entry plan at $29/month (annual; 1 user, 6 profiles) is competitive. Platform support is solid, including Bluesky and Threads. Unique positioning around LinkedIn personal profiles, WordPress publishing, and Google My Business makes it attractive for agencies with diverse client bases. The product is less discovery-oriented than Buffer and less analytics-deep than Sprout, but well-suited to its agency niche.

### Loomly
Loomly is a content calendar and brand management tool built around structured collaboration: calendar views, content approval workflows, and post idea generation ("Post Ideas"). It targets brand teams (marketing departments) rather than agencies. Its 2026 pricing is notably structured into just two main tiers: Starter ($49/month annual, 12 accounts, 3 users) and Beyond ($249/month annual, 60 accounts, unlimited users). Loomly does not have native Bluesky support—users must route through Zapier. Platform support covers Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Google Business, and Threads. Loomly's gap is that it lacks native fediverse/decentralised network support entirely.

### Statusbrew
Statusbrew is an engagement-first social media management platform, distinguishing itself through its social inbox capabilities, rule-based automation (auto-hide/delete comments, assign conversations), and social listening features. It targets mid-market businesses and agencies that have significant community management needs alongside scheduling. Its 2026 pricing starts at $89/month (annual; Lite, 5 profiles, 1 user). Platform support includes Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, TikTok, WhatsApp, YouTube, Reddit, Bluesky, Threads, Google Business, and Pinterest. The inclusion of WhatsApp and Reddit is relatively unusual. Statusbrew occupies a niche between Agorapulse (similar inbox focus) and Sprout Social (analytics depth) in terms of positioning.

### Metricool
Metricool's strongest differentiator is its analytics depth combined with an unusually generous free tier. The free plan supports 20 posts/month across Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky, and Google Business Profile—notably excluding LinkedIn and X by default (those require paid plans or add-ons). This makes Metricool one of only a handful of tools where the free tier includes Bluesky and Threads together. Paid plans start at $16/month (annual; Starter) unlocking LinkedIn and unlimited posts. Metricool also supports advertising management (Facebook Ads, Google Ads, LinkedIn Ads), which is unusual for a scheduling tool at this price point. It targets small businesses, freelancers, and agencies who are analytics-driven without wanting Sprout-level costs.

### Iconosquare
Iconosquare originated as an Instagram analytics tool and still leads with analytics as its core value proposition. By 2026 it supports 8 platforms (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, Pinterest, LinkedIn, X, Threads, YouTube) with strong multi-metric dashboards, competitor tracking, and white-label reporting. It does not support Bluesky. Pricing is in EUR (Launch €33/month, Scale €69/month, Excel €116/month), which creates some friction for non-European users. The free tier (2 profiles, 10 posts/month) is functional but limited. Iconosquare targets agencies and brand managers who prioritise deep social analytics over posting volume or AI features.

### SocialBee
SocialBee's signature feature is its category-based content scheduling and evergreen recycling system. Users can organise posts into categories (e.g. "blog shares", "promotional", "quotes"), assign each category to specific time slots, and configure recycling rules. This makes it particularly valuable for teams with large content libraries who want consistent posting without constant manual curation. SocialBee added Bluesky and Threads support and includes an AI writing suite (unlimited AI generation on all tiers). Pricing starts at $29/month (Bootstrap, 5 profiles) with frequently-offered 50% discounts. It supports 9+ platforms. The category system is a strong differentiator but adds initial setup complexity.

### MeetEdgar
MeetEdgar is the original evergreen content automation tool, founded in 2014 and still focused on its core proposition: a category-based queue that continuously recycles your best content so your posting schedule never runs dry. By 2026 it supports 10 platforms including Bluesky (added in 2025). Pricing is simple: Eddie plan ($24.91/month annual, 5 accounts) and EDGAR plan ($41.58/month annual, 25 accounts). The platform includes Inky, its AI writing assistant, though credits are metered. MeetEdgar targets solopreneurs and small businesses who value consistency and automation over deep analytics. Its age shows in some UI areas but the core automation remains distinctive.

### Publer
Publer is an underrated scheduling tool with unusually flexible per-account pricing (Professional at $12/month for 3 accounts, Business at $21/month for 3 accounts, with additional accounts at ~$4/account). It supports a wide platform set including Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, Pinterest, TikTok, YouTube, Threads, Google Business, Bluesky, Mastodon, Telegram, and WordPress. The X/Twitter integration costs Publer ~$42,000/month in API fees, so it is excluded from the free plan—a unique pricing constraint visible to users. Publer's thread-scheduling feature (supporting multi-post threads on X, Threads, Mastodon, and Bluesky simultaneously) is a noteworthy differentiator. Canva integration is built in. The Business plan adds full analytics. Publer targets budget-conscious users, small agencies, and creators who want wide platform coverage without paying per-seat enterprise prices.

### SocialChamp
SocialChamp is one of the most affordable options in the market with a disruptive pay-per-profile pricing model and a functional free tier (3 accounts, 15 scheduled posts). Its bundled plans start at $29/month (Standard, 6 accounts). Platform support is one of the broadest available: Facebook, X, Instagram, Pinterest, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Google Business, Threads, Bluesky, Mastodon, and WhatsApp Business—12 platforms total. The product quality is competent but the UI and feature depth don't match Hootsuite or Buffer at higher price points. SocialChamp targets price-sensitive users, small businesses, and freelancers who need comprehensive platform coverage cheaply.

### Typefully
Typefully is a text-native, writing-experience-first scheduler that targets writers, journalists, indie developers, and thought leaders rather than brand marketers. It supports only 5 platforms: X/Twitter, LinkedIn, Bluesky, Threads, and Mastodon—deliberately excluding image-heavy platforms like Instagram and TikTok. This narrow focus enables a polished long-form writing experience with thread support, readability tools, and a clean distraction-free composer. The free tier supports 15 posts/month across 1 social set. Paid plans start at ~$8/month (Starter). Typefully is notable as one of the few tools building primarily for the decentralised social web (Bluesky, Mastodon) alongside X and LinkedIn. It is not open source.

### Fedica
Fedica (formerly Tweepsmap) is the specialist analytics and scheduling tool for decentralised and fediverse networks. It supports 12 platforms including Bluesky, Mastodon, Pixelfed, and Tumblr alongside mainstream networks—the broadest fediverse coverage of any tool in this analysis. Fedica's free tier allows 10 scheduled posts at any given time on Bluesky. Paid plans start at $10/month (Publish), $24/month (Grow), and $79/month (Research). The Research tier is notable for its deep audience analytics on decentralised platforms, filling a major gap that mainstream tools (which struggle to aggregate AT Protocol/ActivityPub data) have yet to address. Fedica's target audience is digital privacy advocates, journalists, academics, and brands with fediverse audiences.

### Planable
Planable is not primarily a scheduler—it is a content collaboration and approval platform that happens to include scheduling. Its core value is a visual, Figma-like workspace where teams can see content exactly as it will appear on each platform, leave comments on posts, and manage structured approval flows. It supports Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest, Google Business, and Threads but not Bluesky. Pricing is per-workspace: Basic $33/month (60 posts, 4 profiles), Pro $49/month (150 posts, 10 profiles). Analytics and social inbox are paid add-ons. Planable targets marketing agencies and brand teams for whom internal review and client presentation workflows are the primary bottleneck.

### Postiz (Open Source)
Postiz is the most feature-complete open-source social media scheduler in the space as of 2026. Licensed under AGPL-3.0, it is self-hostable via Docker and has ~30,000 GitHub stars. It supports 20+ platforms including Bluesky, Threads, Mastodon, Reddit, Discord, Telegram, Slack, WordPress, Medium, Twitch, and all mainstream networks. Its cloud SaaS starts at $29/month (Standard, 5 channels). Uniquely, the self-hosted version has feature parity with the cloud version. Postiz also ships its own MCP server and an agent CLI, positioning it at the intersection of social media scheduling and AI agent tooling. Key weaknesses: its breadth means individual platform integrations are sometimes shallower than specialist tools, and the community/documentation is still maturing. It does not have a mobile app.

### Mixpost (Open Source / Self-Hosted)
Mixpost is a Laravel-based self-hosted social media management platform with a distinctive one-time-payment model. The Lite edition (MIT license) is free and open-source, supporting Facebook, X, and Mastodon. The Pro edition ($299 one-time, 1-year updates included) adds Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, TikTok, Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky, and Google Business—11 platforms total. The Enterprise edition ($1,199) adds multi-tenancy and subscription management, making it suitable as a white-label platform. Mixpost is built for developers and technically capable teams who want to run their own social media management infrastructure permanently without recurring SaaS fees. Its AI assistant, approval workflows, and API are solid. The one-time pricing model is a strong differentiator for cost-conscious organisations. There is no mobile app.

### Periwinkle (Adjacent — AT Protocol PDS Hosting)
Periwinkle is not a social media scheduler but is directly relevant to the vibepost market context. It is a Berlin-based startup offering managed hosting for AT Protocol Personal Data Servers (PDS), allowing users to maintain their own Bluesky identity (custom domain handle) and data on their own server without managing the infrastructure themselves. Pricing starts at $4/month. Periwinkle represents the growing segment of users who care deeply about data sovereignty on Bluesky specifically—a user base that naturally overlaps with vibepost's open-source, privacy-focused positioning. Periwinkle is not a competitor but a potential complementary infrastructure partner or integration target.

## 5. Gaps and Opportunities — Where vibepost Can Win

### 5.1 The Open-Source Gap Is Real But Partly Closed

The entry of Postiz (AGPL-3.0, ~30k GitHub stars, cloud + self-host parity) means the "there is no good open-source social scheduler" argument no longer holds. Postiz is the most direct open-source competitor to vibepost and should be studied closely.

However, Postiz's breadth (20+ platforms, AI agent CLI, MCP server) is simultaneously its weakness: it is a complex system with significant infrastructure requirements. Mixpost (MIT/Laravel) is better engineered for single-operator self-hosting but requires one-time purchase for full platform support.

**vibepost's opportunity:** Position as the clean, focused, easy-to-self-host alternative that doesn't require managing a multi-container Docker stack or a paid license for core platforms. GPLv3 (copyleft) combined with deliberately simple deployment could attract the "privacy-first individual" segment that finds Postiz's complexity off-putting.

### 5.2 Bluesky Is Strategically Important and Growing Fast

Bluesky has grown from ~26M users in late 2024 to ~43M by early 2026—a ~65% increase in roughly 18 months. Daily active users are around 4M. Growth has slowed from peak (~5M/month in late 2024) to ~1.6M/month, but the platform is now a legitimate media presence, especially among:
- Journalists and writers (who left X/Twitter)
- Academics and researchers
- LGBTQ+ communities
- Tech professionals and open-source developers
- Progressive political communities

Critically: **Later does not support Bluesky. Loomly does not support Bluesky. Iconosquare does not support Bluesky.** Several major tools—covering a significant share of the mid-market—still treat Bluesky as an afterthought or rely on Zapier workarounds.

Among tools that do support Bluesky (Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout, Agorapulse, SocialPilot, Metricool, SocialBee, Publer, SocialChamp, Fedica, Typefully, Postiz, Mixpost), the implementation quality varies. Many added Bluesky support reactively (late 2024–early 2025) and their Bluesky integrations remain basic: simple post scheduling without native thread support, no AT Protocol-specific features (custom feeds, labellers, PDS integration), and no analytics depth.

**vibepost's opportunity:** Build Bluesky support as a first-class citizen from day one. Native thread support, AT Protocol awareness (custom handles, PDS integration with tools like Periwinkle), and fediverse ethos-aligned positioning would resonate strongly with Bluesky's core user base. No existing tool markets itself as "built *for* Bluesky alongside the rest."

### 5.3 Threads Is a Meaningful Secondary Platform With Gaps

Meta's Threads grew rapidly through 2024–2025 and most serious schedulers now support it (Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout, Agorapulse, SocialPilot, Metricool, SocialBee, Publer, SocialChamp, Sendible, Planable, Postiz, Mixpost). The Threads API is relatively new and some tools' implementations remain limited. Threads support is now table stakes.

**vibepost's opportunity:** This is covered ground but must be included. Focus on quality of Threads cross-posting (multi-image, alttext, thread chains) rather than it being a differentiator.

### 5.4 The Privacy-Focused / Self-Hosted User Is Underserved by UX

Both Postiz and Mixpost are technically capable, but neither has invested heavily in:
- First-run setup experience for non-technical users
- Clean, opinionated UI focused on the cross-posting workflow specifically
- Documentation aimed at individuals (not DevOps teams)
- Clear, non-alarmist privacy messaging about what data leaves your server and when

The self-hosting market has grown significantly in 2025–2026 (driven by data sovereignty concerns, cost fatigue from SaaS subscriptions, and political/platform uncertainty). Tools like Immich (photos), Paperless-ngx (documents), and Actual Budget (finances) show there is a large, enthusiastic self-hosting community willing to run open-source SaaS replacements when the UX is approachable.

**vibepost's opportunity:** Target the r/selfhosted and r/homelab user base alongside indie hackers and privacy advocates. Simple Docker Compose deployment, a polished single-user experience, and explicit data-residency messaging differentiate from Postiz's complexity and Mixpost's developer-tool feel.

### 5.5 The "Five Specific Platforms" Niche Is Defensible

vibepost's stated platform set (Facebook, Instagram, Threads, LinkedIn, Bluesky) is a well-considered, coherent subset. This is exactly the platform combination used by:
- Independent journalists and writers (LinkedIn professional, Bluesky community, Instagram visual)
- Small brands and creators building their "non-X" presence (all five platforms are post-X alternatives)
- Academics and professionals (LinkedIn + Bluesky-heavy)
- Privacy-conscious small businesses exiting X entirely

By being explicit that vibepost is *not* trying to support 20 platforms, the product avoids the maintenance burden and complexity that hobbles larger open-source efforts. Each platform integration can be deeper and better tested.

**Caution:** Not supporting X/Twitter may limit mainstream adoption significantly—X still has ~500M registered users despite its controversies, and many businesses still maintain a presence. Consider whether X exclusion is a firm product decision or a phased roadmap item. TikTok's large user base (especially for creator/brand use cases) is also absent.

### 5.6 Pricing Model Differentiation

The SaaS scheduler market has converged on monthly subscriptions, which creates ongoing cost fatigue for individual users. Mixpost's one-time payment model has been received extremely positively in the self-hosting community. vibepost's GPLv3 license means it must allow self-hosting but does not preclude offering a managed cloud version.

Options:
- **Pure self-hosted GPLv3** (no cloud): Maximises trust and privacy positioning; monetisation relies on donations, enterprise support contracts, or auxiliary services
- **Self-hosted GPLv3 + optional cloud** (Buffer/Postiz model): Sustains development; cloud tier appeals to users who want vibepost without self-hosting
- **One-time license for cloud** (Mixpost model): Strong value proposition for individuals

**vibepost's opportunity:** A "self-host for free forever, cloud at a single flat monthly price with no per-seat gotchas" model (à la SocialChamp's flat bundled pricing rather than Hootsuite's per-user model) would strongly differentiate on pricing transparency.

### 5.7 What the Existing Open-Source Players Are Missing

| Gap | Postiz | Mixpost |
|---|---|---|
| Mobile app | ❌ | ❌ |
| Built-for-Bluesky AT Protocol features | ⚠️ basic | ⚠️ basic |
| Single-user, non-Docker-Compose simple deployment | ❌ | ⚠️ (Laravel package) |
| Privacy-first brand identity | ⚠️ partial | ⚠️ partial |
| GPLv3 license (strong copyleft) | ❌ (AGPL) | ❌ (MIT Lite / commercial Pro) |
| Focused platform set (not 20+) | ❌ (breadth is core value) | ⚠️ |

vibepost sits in none of these gaps alone, but the *combination* of GPLv3, focused platforms, Bluesky-first ethos, and approachable self-hosting UX is not currently occupied by any competitor.

### 5.8 Summary of Key Strategic Recommendations

1. **Bluesky-first is a legitimate differentiator** — No existing tool leads with Bluesky in marketing or product design. The user community that cares about this is large enough to sustain an indie project.

2. **Compete on simplicity against Postiz, not features** — Postiz has more platforms and stars on GitHub. Winning requires being easier to install, easier to use, and having better documentation for non-technical users.

3. **Privacy messaging matters to the target user** — Explicit, verifiable claims about data handling (no analytics beacons, no telemetry by default, all credentials stay on your server) are a meaningful feature for the self-hosted audience.

4. **Mobile app is a real gap** — Neither Postiz nor Mixpost has a mobile app. Even a minimal mobile companion (quick post creation, queue review) would be a significant competitive advantage in the open-source space.

5. **Watch for Later and Loomly adding Bluesky** — If/when they do, the "scheduler that supports Bluesky" angle becomes less distinctive for mainstream tools. vibepost's edge must be the combination of open-source + privacy + Bluesky depth, not Bluesky support alone.

6. **Consider a lightweight approach to analytics** — Most tools' free tiers offer enough analytics to be useful; a self-hosted tool that can export CSV-level engagement data per post without requiring paid upgrades would be appreciated by the target audience.
